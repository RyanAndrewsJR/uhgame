## Goal / feel
- **A1: the sim stays 2D, hidden; a 3D view follows it** (DECISIONS.md, 3D view). Every gameplay number stays as it is, in px and League units. The view converts for display only.
- **Scale: 1 m = 1 tile = 32 px = 100 League units.** The Knight's 375 move speed = 3.75 m/s; the dash 128 px = 4 m; `gameplay_radius` 65 u = 0.65 m.
- **The camera (Ryan's picks after P0b, 2026-10-02):** fixed angle, never rotates, follows the player. Perspective with a 30° field of view, 50° pitch, 28 m wide. That shows about 576 tiles² of floor (+156% over the 2D game's 225), draws a tile about 46 px wide on a 1280 px window (64 today), and makes north–south distances look 23% shorter than east–west (sin 50° = 0.77; accepted, Q11). The numbers don't change, only how they look.
- **Smoothness:** no visible stutter walking along a wall, dashing, or through a hitstop, at 60 Hz and at Ryan's screen rate, **180 Hz: a 5.6 ms frame budget.** P0a: 0.93–1.01× the median speed per frame at 180 Hz, no step back; P0b: 1.8–2.9 ms median frames with the full look.
- **The look (Q4, 2026-10-02):** hand-painted stylized 3D at native resolution, League-style. The material (painted with a light ramp vs fully lit PBR) is open until the art pass (Open questions). Readable lighting first (Q10): soft ambient, one key light with shadows, sparing colored point lights.
- **Characters turn smoothly** to their facing (no 8-direction snap). The Knight's model is 1.8 m tall.
- **Tall things fade:** a building, plateau, rock or wall marked to fade fades (dithered, to 25% over 0.18 s) while it stands between the camera and the player. Walls are 3D assets with their own heights (2.2 m for now), not one cutaway height.
- **Slopes don't change speed.** The sim is flat; walking up a ramp takes as long as the same map distance on flat floor (League's rule).

## Core rules
- **The sim decides, the view shows.** Like VFX and sound events, the view never changes gameplay state.
- **The sim never reads height.** Terrain height lives in the view. The one height-like rule in the sim is the opt-in `elevated` tag (Terrain and height, 2), which comes from where a unit stands.
- **One mapping.** Sim px ↔ view meters go through `Units` only (Architecture, Mapping). No other code multiplies or divides by 32.
- **Views sync on the physics tick, after the sim.** `WorldView`'s `_physics_process` (priority 100, above every sim node's 0) copies every view's position; 3D physics interpolation smooths it. Never copy in `_process`: Node2D has no interpolated getter in 4.7.2, so a copy in `_process` judders.
- **Additive and switchable.** Until the milestone the view sits behind `Main.use_3d_view` (off); with it off the game is exactly today's.
- **Headless tests never build the view.** No test instances `Main`; without a `WorldView` every view hook does nothing. The 1,788 checks keep guarding the same sim.
- **No unanswerable enemy** (Terrain and height, 3).
- **A view outlives its sim node** (a death finishing, an impact playing), and the sim node can be freed first. Every lookup of a view by its sim node checks `is_instance_valid()` before using the entry: copying a freed view into a typed variable stops the game (P0b).
- **Scripted runs never save.** A harness that runs a scene outside `res://scenes/tests/` (screenshots, measurements) sets `Progress.saving_enabled = false` first, or it writes Ryan's `user://progress.cfg` on quit (P0b).

## Rooms (built in 3D; Ryan, 2026-10-02)
Rooms are built from placed 3D assets: floors, walls, rocks, mountains, boulders, grass, buildings. You see the real room while building it. The sim still needs a flat 2D outline for everything that blocks, and that outline is made from the 3D room at load. (Q7's first answer, tile-built rooms with generated 3D, is replaced.)
- **A room layout** (`RoomLayout`, `scripts/rooms/room_layout.gd`) is a scene whose root is a Node3D: `scenes/rooms/<name>.tscn` like today's rooms. Its children are asset scenes (`scenes/rooms/assets/`) placed by hand.
- **A small kit builds many rooms.** Asset pieces are sized in whole meters (a wall 1, 2 or 4 m long; a floor 1×1, 2×2 or 4×4 m), with their origin on a 1 m grid point, and layouts are built with the editor's 1 m snap (90° rotation snap), so pieces line up without gaps. Decoration and rocks may be placed with the snap off.
- **What an asset can carry:**
  - a **footprint** (`Footprint`, a Node3D in the asset's scene): the flat outline the asset occupies in the sim. `kind`: WALL (collision layer 1), LOW_OBSTACLE (7), PIT (6) or LEDGE (11, a manual ledge). `polygon` in meters in the footprint's own X/Z plane. Its surface tags (`grappleable`, `destructible`, `bounce`, `wall_slam`; WORLD_INTERACTION.md) go onto the generated body's `SurfaceTags`.
    - **Convex assets** (rocks, boulders, pillars, crates, a straight wall) may leave `polygon` empty: it's derived as the convex hull of the asset's meshes from the floor up to `slice_height_m` (1.0 m).
    - **Concave assets need drawn polygons, never the hull:** archways, doorways, U-shapes, L-shaped buildings. A hull would close the opening (an archway's hull blocks the arch). Draw the outline, or use several footprints (one per wall of a doorway, two for an L).
  - the group **`walkable`** on a mesh: walkable ground (floors, ramps, plateau tops, a stair's hidden ramp). Its trimesh is the floor pick and the height under each unit.
  - the group **`fades`** on the asset's root: it fades while between the camera and the player.
  - the group **`decoration`** on the asset's root: never in the sim (grass, small rocks, banners). Marked on purpose, so the validator can tell decoration from a forgotten footprint.
- **Markers:** a `PlayerSpawn` (Marker3D) and **sim markers** (`SimMarker`, a Marker3D with `scene: PackedScene`, a 2D sim scene such as an enemy, interactable, hazard, pickup or perch, and `properties: Dictionary` set on the instance, e.g. `passive: true` for a training dummy).
- **Editor tools** (`@tool`, editor only; nothing they make is saved into the scene):
  - `RoomLayout` draws every footprint over the 3D room at floor height, colored by kind (walls red, low obstacles orange, pits purple, ledges yellow; the derived ledges too), updating as assets move. An export turns it off; in play, `debug_draw` shows the same.
  - `SimMarker` shows its scene's model (a unit's `model_scene`, else a labeled capsule), so placed enemies look like themselves in the editor.
- **The validator** (`RoomLayout.validate() -> Array[String]`) runs at load and warns once per problem (`push_warning`); `view_test` runs it on fixture layouts. It flags:
  - an **unmarked solid mesh**: a mesh that is not `walkable`, not part of an asset with a footprint and not `decoration` (it looks solid, but the sim would let you walk through it);
  - an **empty footprint**: fewer than 3 points or no area, or an empty polygon on an asset with no meshes to derive from;
  - a **footprint that doesn't match its meshes**: part of the asset's lower outline (its meshes up to `slice_height_m`) more than 0.25 m (8 px) outside the footprint (a solid thing you can walk into), or footprint area more than 0.25 m from any of its meshes (an invisible wall, such as a hull over an archway).
- **At load** (`RoomLayout.build_sim() -> Room`) the layout makes the sim room: a `Room` (the existing Node2D class) with one StaticBody2D per footprint (its polygon through `Units.m_to_px()`, on its layer, with `SurfaceTags`), the derived ledges (Terrain and height, 1b), `Entities` filled from the sim markers (positions through `Units.to_sim()`), `PlayerSpawn`, and the navigation bake (parses layers 1, 6, 7 and 11). Main then uses that `Room` exactly as it uses a tile room, and the layout itself is the room's look under `WorldView`. `Room` and `Main` read the `Tiles` layer today for the bake outline and the camera bounds; a room without tiles gives its bounds instead (the walkable ground's extent, or an export).
- **Tile rooms keep working.** With the view on, `RoomView` gives them a placeholder look generated from the tiles (P3). New rooms are layouts.
  - **Test fixtures stay tile rooms:** the tile `sandbox.tscn` (which the test suites instance) and every test's room. Players get a separate 3D sandbox layout (P8).
  - **`room_01` is rebuilt as a layout, the last item of P8** (Ryan, 2026-10-02). The tile version stays the game's room until the layout matches it (same walls, enemies and spawn; Ryan checks), then Main switches to the layout, and the tile version is disabled and later deleted with his OK.
- **Built in P8** (see CHANGELOG.md; `scripts/rooms/room_layout.gd`, `footprint.gd`, `sim_marker.gd`):
  - **A footprint belongs to its parent:** a `Footprint` is a child of its asset's root, and the asset's meshes are that root's other descendants. A mesh is covered when an ancestor (below the layout) has a Footprint child.
  - **A derived outline** is the convex hull of the asset's solid meshes cut to the footprint's slice: from its height up to `slice_height_m`, or for a pit, down to it. Walls, rocks and the fence are derived; the archway (one footprint per pillar), the L-shaped building and the pit are drawn.
  - **Surface tags wait for `SurfaceTags`** (WORLD_INTERACTION.md), which isn't built. Footprints have no tag exports until then.
  - **`build_sim()`:**
    - A `Room` (no Tiles) named after the layout. Its `Footprints` node (in `navigation_source`) holds one StaticBody2D per footprint, on its kind's layer, colliding with nothing, its outline in px, with a flat 2D look so the room plays without the view.
    - `Entities` gets each `SimMarker`'s scene, named after the marker, its `properties` set. `PlayerSpawn` comes from a `PlayerSpawn` Marker3D child.
    - The layout's children that aren't Node3D (room scripts such as the sandbox's helpers) move into the Room.
    - `Room.bounds_px` is the walkable ground's extent, or `bounds_m`.
    - `Room.get_bounds_px()` (the used tiles, or `bounds_px`) is now the bake's outline and Main's camera bounds. The bake carves layers 1, 6, 7 and 11 (`Room.NAV_BLOCKING_LAYERS`); a tile room has only layer 1, so its bake is unchanged.
  - **The layout is read before it's in the tree:** positions come from local transforms (`RoomLayout.transform_in()`), and the root stays at the origin.
  - **Main:** a `room_scene` whose root is a `RoomLayout` is built with `build_sim()`. With the view on, `WorldView.setup(..., layout)` moves the layout under the view and adds its floor pick: one ConcavePolygonShape3D of every walkable mesh, on 3D layer 1. Its walkable floors' `floor_drawings` materials take the floor drawings, and its `fades` assets fade. With the view off, Main warns and frees the layout; the footprints' flat look is the room.
  - **The validator, built as specified, plus three rules found in P8:**
    - A point inside a closed mesh's cross-section (at 10, 50 and 90% of the slice) counts as covered, so the middle of a boulder isn't an "invisible wall". A hull over an archway's opening still is.
    - Pits skip the "invisible wall" check; a pit is a hole.
    - An asset scene placed many times is checked once.
    - Measured: room_01's layout (98 wall pieces, 256 floor pieces) validates in 13 ms and builds its sim in 23 ms.
  - **The editor tools** (`@tool`; their looks are internal children marked `RoomLayout.TOOL_META`, never saved):
    - Each `Footprint` draws its own outline over the room, so it follows the asset as it moves. A derived one is redrawn every half second in the editor. `RoomLayout.show_footprints` turns them off; in play they're drawn only with `debug_draw`.
    - A `SimMarker` shows its scene's `model_scene`, or a capsule as wide as its gameplay radius, tinted like its 2D body, with the marker's name.
  - **The placeholder kit** (`scenes/rooms/assets/`, materials in `materials/`): every piece in whole meters, its origin at its corner on the floor (min x, min z), running along +x and +z. Turned 90° (local +x along +z), a piece placed at x covers x − 1 to x.
    - Floors `floor_1x1`, `floor_2x2`, `floor_4x4` (walkable; `floor_stone`, the floor shader with a 1 m checker).
    - Walls `wall_1m`, `wall_2m`, `wall_4m` (2.2 m) and `wall_4m_tall` (3.4 m), each 1 m thick, in `fades`.
    - `archway_4m` (a 2 m opening), `building_l` (6 × 6 m with a 3 × 3 m notch, 3 m walls, a roof), `fence_4m` (low obstacle), `pit_2x2` (0.9 m deep), `rock_large` (a boulder; origin at its middle), `rock_small` (pebbles, decoration).
    - `lighting_default` (the environment and key light, RoomView's numbers): a layout places one, so it's lit in the editor as in play.
  - **The floor's shader** gained `albedo`, `albedo_alt` and `checker_m` for kit floors without vertex colors. RoomView leaves them white and off, so its look is unchanged.
  - **The rooms:**
    - `scenes/rooms/sandbox_3d.tscn` is the 3D sandbox: 30 × 20 m like the tile one, with an archway partition, a tall wall, the building, the fence, the pit and rocks. It has the tile sandbox's dummies, enemies, spawn and four helper nodes at the same points. Play it from `scenes/sandbox_main_layout.tscn` (Ryan, 2026-10-03).
    - `scenes/rooms/room_01_layout.tscn` was generated from room_01's tiles: rows of wall cells in 4, 2 and 1 m pieces, then columns, then single cells; floors in 4, 2 and 1 m squares. Play it from `scenes/main_layout.tscn`. Main still plays the tile room_01 until Ryan confirms the match.
  - **Fences block walking now; pits wait** (Ryan, 2026-10-03). The player's and slimes' walking masks gained layer 7: a fence stops walking and knockback (`displace()` keeps the mask) but not the dash (`collision_mask & 1`) or projectiles. Pit footprints are carved from the navigation (enemies path around them), but units can walk over a pit until the pit step (WORLD_INTERACTION.md, Pits and movement types).

## Wing scale (DUNGEONS.md; Ryan 2026-10-03)
A dungeon's wing is one run: about 40–60 distinct, hand-built **spaces** (a hall, a bridge, a courtyard, a cavern) and 45–90 minutes of play, over one or more **floors** (a wing's storeys) joined by stairs, ladders, lifts or pit-drops with a brief fade (DUNGEONS.md, Structure). It's sized by spaces, play time and measured authoring cost, never by area.
- **The sim is still hidden 2D `Room`(s).** Every space is built in 3D as this doc's Rooms section says (kit pieces, footprints, sim markers, the validator), and the sim the game plays is the flat 2D Room made from them. Nothing about A1 changes: the sim decides, the view shows.
- **Floors are separate rooms** (Terrain and height 1c). No seamless streaming between floors in v1: a floor change fades out, builds the floor's sim, and fades in.
- **Inside a floor, the P-spike decides** between two shapes:
  - **one sim Room per floor**, its spaces placed side by side in one layout, so you walk between them with no load (far enemies asleep, below); or
  - **one Room per space**, joined by doorways with a short fade, as floors are.
- **What grows with a wing and what doesn't:**
  - doesn't grow: `FloorOverlay` (a window around the camera, whatever the room's size: P7), `ScreenOverlay` (per unit on screen), the camera and the fade;
  - grows: the sim's physics (P0a: 50 extra slimes take 10–12 ms of physics step), WorldView's per-tick sync of every view, the navigation mesh (its bake and its path queries), the meshes and lights the renderer must cull, and the time `build_sim()` takes at a floor change (room_01's layout: 23 ms for 354 pieces).
  - So a floor-sized Room needs **sleeping**: units and views beyond a distance from the player stop processing (physics, AI, view sync) until the player comes near. The sleep distance sits beyond the distance at which packs notice the player (ENEMIES_AI.md).
- **Backdrops** (DUNGEONS.md, Scale): distant geometry that is **view only**, there to show the wing's scale: the castle you came through, visible below a high bridge; a skyline; a shaft dropping to a lower floor. A backdrop:
  - is in the `decoration` group, so the validator knows it's meant to have no footprint;
  - is never `walkable`, so it's never on the view-only floor layer: the floor pick and the height ray never land on it, the camera's focus never follows it, and FloorOverlay's `drop_m` (the walkable height range) ignores it;
  - may reuse kit pieces and art from earlier spaces (usually a cheaper copy), but never instances another space's `RoomLayout`, whose footprints and sim markers would enter the sim;
  - may sit outside `Room.bounds_px` and below or above the walkable ground; where the camera looks past a floor's edge (`GameCamera3D`: bounds keep the focus on the floor, so the void past it shows), the backdrop fills the void;
  - is a candidate for Godot's visibility ranges (distance fade and level of detail), measured in the P-spike.
- **Long sightlines** are limited by the fixed camera (about 22 m of floor in view, 13 m beyond the focus): height and backdrops carry the sense of distance more than the floor in view does.

## Terrain and height
Ryan's givens: real terrain height like Diablo IV (stairs, ramps, hills, plateaus), knock-ups, no jumping, and a melee champion can always answer an elevated enemy. Under A1 these are three separate things.

### 1a. Airborne (knock-up): a status plus a view-only arc
- **Status** `status_airborne.tres`: tags `cc`, `airborne`, `debuff`. Blocks moving, attacking, casting and dashing (a hard CC, like the stun). Stack rule REFRESH_LONGER. Its duration comes from the hit that applies it (typically 0.4–1.0 s).
- **Tenacity doesn't shorten it** (League's rule: the arc keeps its shape): new StatusEffect field `ignores_tenacity` (default false).
- **I-frames** (dash, post-hit) block the whole hit, so they block a knock-up. Being airborne gives no i-frames: juggles work.
- **A cleanse doesn't end it; unstoppable doesn't end it** (Ryan, 2026-10-01): new StatusEffect field `cleansable` (default true; airborne false), skipped by `StatusComponent.remove_statuses_with_tags()`. Unstoppable still refuses a new one (it's `cc`).
- **Enemies don't knock up the player in v1** (VISION's first priority: control is never taken without a clear reason). Only telegraphed boss attacks are candidates later (ENEMIES_AI.md). The player's own abilities knock up enemies.
- **Displacement:** a knock-back-and-up is one hit with a `knockback_px` and `status_airborne`. While airborne and displaced, a unit ignores low obstacles, ledges and pits (its mask drops layers 7, 11 and 6); walls still stop it. When it lands: over a pit, the pit rule; inside a low obstacle or a ledge's footprint, `WorldQuery.resolve_valid_position()` puts it on the nearest floor on the side it came from (since the P9 fix: the shortest way out, and only the body over a ledge leaves it on the ground its center is over; Built in P9, Landing). No fall damage (Ryan, 2026-10-01). Airborne blocks dashing, so a knock-up can't be dashed out of.
- **Reaction rules** use what exists: `STATUS_APPLIED` with required tag `airborne`; `HIT` rules and `target:airborne` scopes read it from `ctx.target_tags`.
- **View:** the model rises and falls on a curve over the status's actual duration (apex and curve are view data).

### 1b. Walkable height: the view's, never the sim's
- **Source:** the layout's `walkable` meshes. A view's height is the walkable ground under its sim position (a downward ray on the view-only floor layer). Stairs carry a hidden smooth ramp as their walkable mesh, so models glide instead of popping a step at a time; the visible steps are decoration.
- **The view reads height for:** model Y, the camera's focus height, floor drawings (the floor shader follows the slope), projectile height (moves smoothly from its start height toward the terrain under its target, never below it) and the floor pick.
- **The sim keeps its flat footprint.** Where the walkable ground steps by more than 0.3 m with no ramp or stairs (a cliff), the layout makes a **ledge**: a collider on layer 11, derived once at load from the walkable meshes. Ledges block walking and dashes, and navigation (the bake parses 11), but not projectiles or line of sight (`WorldQuery` and `Sight` mask layer 1 only). Ramps and stairs are plain floor in the sim.
- **Why a layer of its own** (Ryan, 2026-10-01, option b): the ghosted dash (dash, Lunge, Triple Step) keeps only walls in its mask while it runs; on layer 7 it would climb any cliff. So the ghosted dash keeps layers 1 and 11 (`collision_mask & (1 | 1024)`; an approved one-line replace in `movement_component.gd`), and fences and rubble stay on 7, crossable by dashes. Airborne units drop 11 too.
- Later option, off by default: Godot's one-way collision could make a ledge passable downward only.

### 1c. Overlapping floors are separate rooms
One floor per map spot. A bridge with enemies under it, a balcony over a hall or a two-story building are separate rooms joined by stairs or doors (Ryan, Q13).

### 2. The default hit rule and the opt-in `elevated` tag
- **Default:** hits use 2D distance, whatever the terrain. An enemy at a plateau's edge can be hit from below by anything that reaches it, melee included (League's rule).
- **Opt-in, for special enemies** (snipers, tower archers): the tag `elevated` from `status_elevated`. A **perch** (a Hazard-style Area2D placed with a sim marker) gives it while a unit stands inside and removes it on exit; the player gets it too on the perch. (WORLD_INTERACTION.md's Hazard spec gets the "while inside" option.)
- **One filter:** `AbilityUtil.can_reach(attacker, target, hit_tags) -> bool`: an `elevated` target can't be hit by a hit tagged `melee` unless the attacker is `elevated` too. Called in two places only: AbilityUtil's target picks (melee aim help never snaps to a target it can't hit) and `HitPipeline.resolve()` (the last guard). Never inside abilities.
- **Hit tag `melee`:** MELEE combo swings and abilities tagged `melee` (a new standard ability tag). The Knight: **Cleave and Lunge**. Not **Judgement** (UNIT-targeted, 450 u = 4.5 m, walks into range; Ryan, 2026-10-01) and not **Cleave Wave** (a 7 m projectile).

### 3. No unanswerable enemy
- Every elevated enemy has at least one answer: a **walk-up route**, a **pull or grapple**, a **ranged answer** (an ability without `melee` that reaches it from walkable floor), or a **dead zone** at its base (it can't attack units close to its perch).
- Every champion has at least one answer to every elevated enemy; a walk-up route counts. **The Knight:** walk-up routes; Judgement (needs walkable floor within 4.5 m of the perch); Cleave Wave (with the item). He has no pull.
- Each perch notes its answers; the test sandbox's perch has all of them.

### Built in P9 (see CHANGELOG.md)
With Ryan's answers at P9's start (2026-10-03): pits stay their own step (a knock-up just carries a unit across a pit until then), the perch holds a passive dummy (a sniper's shot and dead zone come with ENEMIES_AI.md), no pull ability, and the Knight's test knock-up is the Uppercut on W in the 3D sandbox only.
- **Airborne:**
  - **The status:** `status_airborne.tres` holds the data above. `StatusEffect.ignores_tenacity` and `cleansable` are built: StatusComponent skips tenacity for the first, and `remove_statuses_with_tags()` skips a status that isn't cleansable. So neither a cleanse nor gaining unstoppable ends a knock-up, though unstoppable still refuses a new one.
  - **Its movement:** while a unit is airborne and displaced, `MovementComponent` drops layers 6, 7 and 11 for that move (`AIRBORNE_IGNORED_MASK`; `is_airborne()`).
  - **Landing:** when the displacement ends, if the unit's body overlaps a wall, a low obstacle or a ledge, `WorldQuery.resolve_valid_position()` moves it the shortest way out (`push_out()`). The unit's center decides where it lands:
    - **Only its body overlaps** (it lands on the stairs with its edge over their side's ledge, or beside a fence): it stays on the ground its center is over, and the move never takes its center across anything.
    - **Its center is inside** (a fence, a ledge's 0.25 m strip): the move never goes farther along the push, so it comes out on the side it came from. A 2 m push from the middle of the sandbox's plateau ends on the cliff's rim, inside its ledge, so the unit lands back on top, just inside it. From the edge, it goes off and lands below.
    - **No way out within twice its radius:** P9's first rule, a step back along the push line toward where it started.
    - **Found at Ryan's P9 check (fixed 2026-10-03):** the ramp and stairs are 2 m wide, but their side ledges leave a slime's center only a 0.6 m band where its body touches neither. P9's first rule sent a landing outside that band back along the whole push, often to where it started.
  - **The arc:** in `UnitView`, `airborne_apex_m` (1.2) and `data/curves/curve_airborne.tres` (0 at both ends, 1 half way) run over the status's actual duration, interpolated between ticks. A knock-up refreshed mid-air starts a new arc from the current height. While airborne the model plays the stun clip, and what sits over its head rises with it (`WorldView.unit_top_m()`).
  - **The test knock-up:** `test_w_uppercut.tres` is a 2 m cone that knocks enemies up for 0.75 s and back 2 m; `SandboxAbilities.test_w` sets it in `sandbox_3d.tscn`.
- **Height in the view:**
  - **The ray:** `WorldView.ground_height_m(px)` casts a downward ray on the floor layer (the floor pick's bodies), during the physics tick.
  - **Following it:** `EntityView.ground_height_m()` follows that height at up to 8 m/s (`ground_speed_m_per_s`). That's faster than any walkable slope (a ramp climbs about 1.4 m/s at the Knight's speed), so ramps and stairs are followed exactly while a fall off a cliff drops smoothly instead of popping.
  - **Projectiles** start 0.9 m over the ground where they're fired and ease toward 0.9 m over the ground under them, 15% a tick, never closer than 0.15 m to it.
  - **The camera's focus** is the player view's height. FloorOverlay's window also covers floor `drop_m` lower (the layout's walkable height range), which is seen farther.
  - **The floor's shader** fades drawings on faces steeper than about 45°, so a telegraph doesn't smear down a cliff or a stair's riser.
- **Derived ledges** (`RoomLayout.ground_grid()`, `derive_ledges()`):
  - The walkable meshes' faces are rasterized onto a 0.25 m grid, keeping the topmost height per cell. No physics is needed, so it works before the layout is in the tree.
  - A cell is a ledge cell when a neighbor is lower by more than 0.3 m. That's the top side of a cliff, so a unit on top stops 0.25 m inside the edge, and one below stops at the face.
  - Each row's ledge cells merge into rectangles. `build_sim()` puts them in one `Ledges` body (layer 11) under `Footprints`, which the bake carves.
  - Ramps and stairs (a hidden ramp) are smooth, so they make no ledges, except along their sides where they stand more than 0.3 m high. Where the ground just ends (the room's edge, a wall's gap), there's no ledge.
  - In the editor, `RoomLayout` draws them yellow, recomputed only when the walkable meshes move; in play, with `debug_draw`.
  - The walking masks went to 1095 (ledges added). The ghosted dash keeps walls and ledges (`MovementComponent.GHOST_KEEP_MASK`).
- **A cliff face is decoration.** Its place in the sim is the derived ledge, so it needs no footprint.
  - The kit: `plateau_4x4` (a walkable top 1.5 m up and a decoration cliff), `ramp_4x2` (1.5 m over 4 m, 20.6°), `stairs_3x2` (six 0.25 m steps as decoration, with the floor material so drawings show on their tops, over a hidden walkable ramp), and materials `cliff_rock` and `floor_stone_plain`. `get_floor_materials()` now takes every mesh using the floor shader, not only walkable ones.
  - The 3D sandbox's terrain corner:
    - a plateau against the north wall (x 12..16, z 1..5);
    - a ramp up from the east (x 16..20, z 2..4) and stairs up from the south (x 12..14, z 5..8);
    - a perch over the whole top, with its answers noted;
    - a passive dummy on it.
- **The perch and the melee rule:**
  - **`Perch`** (`scripts/world/perch.gd`, `scenes/world/perch.tscn`; an Area2D with mask player and enemies, `size_m`, `status`, `answers`) gives `status_elevated` (the tag `elevated`, until removed, IGNORE) while a unit's feet are inside, and takes it away when they leave.
  - **`AbilityUtil.can_reach()`** is called in its two places:
    - `HitPipeline.resolve()` blocks a melee hit on an elevated target from a unit that isn't elevated;
    - the combo's aim help (`AutoAttackComponent._find_assist_target()`) skips such a target, which is the melee aim help the rule names.
  - No melee UNIT-targeted ability exists, so the UNIT target pick isn't filtered.
  - **The melee tag:** `melee` is in Cleave's and Lunge's data, and `HitPipeline.basic_attack()` adds it to a MELEE combo's swings.
  - **Enemy basic attacks** (the slimes') aren't tagged melee, so they can hit an elevated player. Revisit with ENEMIES_AI.md.
- **Judgement from below:** its walk into range follows a navigation path. To a target on a navmesh island (a plateau with no way up), the path ends at the nearest reachable spot, so the Knight stops below in range and casts.
  - Measured in the sandbox: he stopped at z 7.66 m, below the cliff, and the hit landed.
  - Engine fact: a path query to an unreachable point returns the path to the closest reachable point.

### What A1 can't do (A2, a full 3D port, is the escape hatch)
Gravity (nothing falls by physics), height-dependent hit rules beyond `elevated` (high-ground bonus, parapets), arcing projectiles over walls (an arc is only a look), overlapping floors in one room, true 3D line of sight. If the game needs one, A2 reuses this whole view layer.

## Architecture / contracts
### Mapping (`units.gd`)
`Units.PX_PER_METER` (32.0), `px_to_m()`, `m_to_px()`, `to_view(p: Vector2, height_m := 0.0) -> Vector3` (x → x, y → z), `to_sim(p: Vector3) -> Vector2`. The `_m` suffix means meters, as `_px` means pixels.

### `WorldView` (Node3D under Main; `scripts/view/world_view.gd`)
Holds the environment, the key light, the camera, the room's look and every view. `process_physics_priority` 100. Its `_physics_process` syncs every view after the sim moved. It listens to `SceneTree.node_added` for new sim nodes. A view whose sim node moved more than 64 px in one tick is snapped with `reset_physics_interpolation()` (a teleport; the dash peaks near 23 px a tick).

### The generic view mechanism
- A sim node with a look joins the group `view_source` and has a `view_scene: PackedScene`. `WorldView` instances the view scene under itself and calls `setup(sim_node)`.
  - **Built in P6:** `view_scene` is an export, and null means the node's default.
  - The node answers `get_view_scene()`, which loads that default with `load()` only when a WorldView asks. A sim script never preloads a view scene: the view's script names the sim's classes and WorldView, so a preload would be a compile-time cycle. The 2D game and the tests never load a view.
  - WorldView hears new nodes through `SceneTree.node_added` and builds the view once the node is ready (nodes join the group in their `_ready()`).
  - It loads the default view scenes once when it starts (`DEFAULT_VIEW_SCENES`). The first stun, aura or staggered mark of a fight otherwise loaded its scene from disk on that frame: 19–24 ms.
  - View sources so far: `Unit`, `Projectile`, `aura.gd`, `stun_stars.gd`, `staggered_mark.gd`. Planned: the companion's `CompanionComponent` (a `Node2D` point, not a Unit), whose default is `CompanionView` (COMPANIONS.md, CO2).
  - A status VFX's view follows the unit it sits on, over its model (`WorldView.unit_height_m()`), not the 2D node's own position: that's drawn above the head in screen space, so in the sim it's north of the unit.
- The view root extends **`EntityView`** (Node3D): `sync()` follows the sim node (position through `Units.to_view()`, height from the terrain, facing if the node has one); `on_sim_exited()` runs when the sim node leaves the tree (default: free; a unit finishes its death, a projectile plays its impact).
- Covered this way: units (**`UnitView`**), projectiles (a default bolt tinted by the ability's `icon_color`), auras, status VFX (`stun_stars.tscn`, `staggered_mark.tscn`), pickups and hazards when they're built, and presentation-hook scenes (`VFX.spawn_scene()` accepts a Node3D root).
  - **Hook scenes, built in P7:** a scene whose root is a Node3D goes under the WorldView (`add_scene_at()`): its origin on the floor at the hook's point, its +Z along the hook's angle (as a model faces its facing), its root's own transform kept relative to that. Without a view (the 2D game, every test) it isn't spawned. A Node2D root spawns in the sim as before, and shows on the 3D floor if it's on canvas visibility layer 3.
  - **Sim code reaches the view** through `WorldView.of(node)` (the group `world_view`; null without a view): a damage number, a 3D hook scene. Not a field on Unit: Player already has `world_view`.
- **`UnitView`:** the model (`model_scene` or a capsule), smooth turning to the facing (exponential, rate 20/s; P0b), the hit flash (from `damaged`), death (from `died`, finishing after the Unit is freed), the dash and i-frame looks, swing and cast clips through the hooks (Data, Animation), and the airborne arc. A glTF model faces +Z: a sim facing (x, y) is the yaw `atan2(x, y)`; looping clips need `loop_mode` set.
  - **Built in P6.** The placeholder capsule is as wide as the gameplay radius and 2.4 times as tall (a slime: 0.55 m, 1.32 m), tinted like the 2D body, with a nub showing its facing.
  - An enemy without a facing turns to its walk, or to the target it winds up at. A placeholder squashes during a windup or cast and stretches on the hit.
  - The hit flash is an additive overlay (`material_overlay`, `flash_overlay.gdshader`), so it works over any model's own materials.
  - The post-hit i-frames blink the model like the 2D body.
  - **Dash afterimages** use the 2D MovementVFXComponent's numbers (count 4, every 0.03 s, fading over 0.15 s), so both looks tune in one place.
    - A rigged model keeps a pool of copies made at load, each posed with the model's clip at its current time.
    - Baking a skinned mesh's pose (`bake_mesh_from_current_skeleton_pose()`) cost up to 40 ms a dash. It also fails headless, since skinning lives in the renderer.
- Without a `WorldView` (every test), nothing spawns.

### The room's look
- A layout room is its own look (Rooms). **`RoomView`** builds the placeholder look of a tile room from its tiles: floor, wall blocks (2.2 m, in `fades`), environment, the key light and shadows. P3. A box per wall cell, each its own `fades` asset, so only the cells in the line of sight fade.
- **The 2D world is hidden from the screen, not from physics:** the room's root and `Entities` go on canvas layer 2 "sim", `Main` on 1 and 2 (every CanvasItem ancestor of a drawing must share a layer with a viewport's mask), and the root viewport's `canvas_cull_mask` drops layer 2. A hidden CharacterBody2D collides identically (P0a, 0.000000 px; P3: a scripted walk with the view on and off, identical frame for frame). The root viewport outlives the scene, so `WorldView` restores its mask when it leaves the tree.
- **Fading:** each `fades` asset fades while the segment from the camera to the player's feet, chest or head (0, 0.9 and 1.8 m up) crosses its bounds. At the default look a 2.2 m wall hides the player when it stands within about 2 m south of them. The fade is dithered in the shader (a 4×4 ordered pattern; skipped in the shadow pass), so the asset stays opaque and keeps its full shadow; the amount is an `instance uniform`, one material for every instance (P0b).

### `GameCamera3D` (Camera3D; `scripts/camera/game_camera_3d.gd`)
- Follows in `_process` from the player view's `get_global_transform_interpolated()`, its own physics interpolation off (P0a). Its look comes from `CameraLook`; the distance (perspective) or `size` (orthographic) is recomputed from the viewport's aspect every frame, so the visible width holds at any window shape.
- **`GameCamera`'s behaviors carry over:** lock on Y, hold C to center, edge and arrow pan, the aim lean with its dead zone, curve and hold time, room bounds, shake through `GameFeel.shake()`, `snap_to_target()` with interpolation resets. The lean is measured in screen space (`GameCamera.get_current_lead()`), so the same share of the screen moves the 3D focus (P0b); shake is screen px of the 640×360 canvas, converted to `h_offset` / `v_offset`.
  - **One place to tune them (P4):** until the 2D camera goes (P-M, asked separately), `GameCamera` stays where they're tuned and computed. `GameCamera3D` reads its `locked`, `get_current_lead()`, `offset` (the shake), `edge_pan_speed`, `edge_margin` and follow smoothing.
  - **The lean puts the target exactly `lead` screen px off the center,** as in 2D: the focus is `target − screen_to_floor(−lead)`. Not `target + screen_to_floor(lead)`: up and down the screen aren't symmetric in perspective (3 px off at 48 px; P4). `screen_to_floor(offset_px)` is the floor distance between the points under the center and under the center plus the offset: 80 px sideways = 3.5 m. It's computed with the shake set aside, since `v_offset` moves the camera along its tilted up axis.
  - **Bounds keep the focus on the room's floor** (Ryan, P4): the point the camera looks at, after the lean or a pan, never leaves the room. Near an edge the void past the walls shows. The 2D rule (nothing outside the room shows) would freeze the camera in rooms about the view's size.
  - **The follow smoothing:** Camera2D moves `speed × tick` of the way on each physics tick; `GameCamera3D` uses the same pace per second (speed 10 at 60 Hz = 10.94/s), on game time, so hitstop freezes it as it freezes Camera2D. Panning uses real time, as in `GameCamera`.
  - **Order each frame:** `GameCamera` (process priority 0), then `GameCamera3D` (10), then `WorldView`'s fade (20), which looks from where the camera is this frame.
- **The listener:** an `AudioListener2D` (made current) follows the camera's floor focus in px every tick, and `Audio.get_listener_position()` reads it. Panning stays right because the camera never rotates.
  - **Sounds scale with the view** (Ryan, P4): while the listener is current, `Audio.distance_scale` is the view's width over the canvas's (28 m = 896 px over 640: 1.4). Each positional sound's reach is `max_distance_px` × the scale and its panning strength ÷ the scale, so a sound at the screen's edge is as loud and as panned as in 2D. The data and the 2D game are unchanged; the scale is back to 1 when the camera leaves.

### Floor drawings: `FloorOverlay` (the plan's `GroundOverlay`, renamed)
A SubViewport sharing the sim's World2D (`world_2d` = the root's), `canvas_cull_mask` = layers 2 and 3, transparent, at 2 texels per sim px (64 per meter). Its `canvas_transform` is set after it enters the tree (before, Godot errors with `canvas_map.has`).
- **It covers a window around the camera, not the whole room,** so its cost doesn't grow with room size. The window is the floor the camera sees, plus a 2 m margin. Each frame its origin moves with the camera, snapped to whole texels so drawings don't shimmer, through the `canvas_transform`. The floor's shader gets the window's origin and size. When the room is smaller than the window, the window is clamped to the room.
- **The size at the default look** (perspective, 30° field of view, 50° pitch, 28 m wide, 16:9): the camera sits 29.4 m from its focus, 22.5 m up. It sees a floor trapezoid 22.9 m wide at the screen's bottom edge and 36.1 m at the top. It reaches from 8.4 m in front of the focus to 13.3 m beyond it, 21.7 m deep.
  - With the margin, the window is 40.1 × 25.7 m: **2624 × 1664 texels** (rounded up to multiples of 64), **4.4 million texels, 16.7 MB** of video memory (RGBA8).
  - A different look recomputes it from the camera frustum. The frustum is taken at the lowest walkable height in view: a floor 1 m lower is seen 1.4 m farther at the top edge.
  - For comparison, the 30 × 20 m sandbox is 1920 × 1280 at the same density (smaller than the window, so the clamp applies). That's what P0b drew every frame, inside a whole-frame GPU time of 0.3–0.85 ms.
- **Cost, estimated:** each frame the window is cleared and the few drawings on screen are drawn into it. At 4.4 million texels that should be well under 0.1 ms of GPU time on Ryan's RTX 4070 SUPER. P7 measures it on its own (the spikes only measured whole frames). Telegraphs, swing arcs, auras and the Player's ability indicators draw into it, and the **floor's shader samples it at each surface point's x/z**: a vertical projection that follows slopes and fades on cliff faces. The overlay is premultiplied (drawn over a transparent target), so the shader un-premultiplies it. Measured in P0a: within the measurement's resolution of the true hit edge on a ramp. A Decal can't take a ViewportTexture in 4.7.2 (a per-frame copy costs 3.2 ms), and a flat quad was 11–13 px off on a slope.
- **Built in P7** (`scripts/view/floor_overlay.gd`; see CHANGELOG.md):
  - **What draws into it:** a 2D node on canvas visibility layer 3 (`FloorOverlay.DRAWING_VISIBILITY_BIT`). The drawings stay drawn by their own 2D nodes: `Telegraph`, `VFX.ring()` and `VFX.slash()`, and a unit's own drawing (its hover ring, the Player's ability indicators; `Unit._ready()` adds the bit). The unit's children (its 2D body, its bar) stay on layer 1, so the overlay culls them. The 2D game draws every layer, so the bit changes nothing there. Auras and status VFX are views (P6), not floor drawings.
  - **The window:** the floor seen around the focus is measured from the screen's four corners with `GameCamera3D.screen_to_floor()` (shake aside), plus `margin_m`. The texture is resized only when the screen's shape changes. Each frame (process priority 25, after the camera) the window's corner follows the focus, the room clamps it, and it snaps to whole texels. Only then do the canvas transform and the floor material's `drawings_origin_m` change.
  - **The floor's shader** (`floor_drawings.gdshader`): the floor's own color (its vertex colors) with the overlay sampled straight down at x/z. The drawings are unlit: un-premultiplied, to linear, and added as emission over the floor, which they cover by their alpha. So a telegraph reads the same in a shadow. Its sampler defaults to transparent, so a floor without an overlay looks plain.
  - **Floor circles are true circles under the view.** The 2D game squashes a floor circle to 0.55 north–south for its 3/4 look; the 3D camera foreshortens the floor itself. `VFX.floor_squash` is 0.55 in 2D and 1 while a WorldView shows the game (`flatten_floor_drawings()`, restored when it leaves). The hover ring and `VFX.ring()` read it; `Telegraph` was always a true circle.
  - **Measured:** the overlay's own GPU time is 0.014 ms a frame (median; max 0.04 ms), and its CPU render time 0.05 ms. A slam-sized telegraph's outline lies on its sim circle within 0.41 window px, under 1 cm on the floor.

### `ScreenOverlay` (CanvasLayer)
Damage numbers and health bars, placed with `Camera3D.unproject_position()`. Under `canvas_items` stretch that works in the 640×360 canvas space, the same space as the mouse and every CanvasLayer (P0b). `Unit._add_number()` gets the overlay path while the old path stays for the 2D game.
- **Built in P7** (`scripts/view/screen_overlay.gd`, CanvasLayer 0: under the HUD's 1 and the pause menu's 10; process priority 30, after the camera):
  - **Health bars:** each unit's view gets a copy of its 2D `HealthBar` (the same exports and look), fed by its HealthComponent (`HealthBar.health`). The bar sits `bar_gap_px` (3) over the top of the model, where the model is drawn that frame. It's shown while the 2D bar is (Unit hides it at death) and goes with the unit.
  - **Damage numbers:** the DamageNumber Label the unit makes, held at a fixed point in the world: `number_height_share` (0.8) of the model's height over where the unit stood, with the 2D sideways spread. It pops, rises and fades as in 2D, and stays where it appeared as the camera moves, as a number in the 2D world does.
  - **Sizes:** the 2D canvas sizes, so numbers read as they were tuned (COMBAT.md). `number_scale` (1) scales them if they don't.

### The floor pick and aim (P5)
- `Player.get_aim_point()` returns the floor pick when a view exists, else the mouse as today; the 10 direct mouse calls in `player.gd` go through it (approved 2026-10-01). The player reaches the view through `Player.world_view`, set by `WorldView.setup()`; null without a view, so tests and the 2D game take the mouse path unchanged (P5).
- **Past the walkable ground, the plane** (P5): where the pick misses, the aim is the ray's point on the flat plane at the camera focus's height (`WorldView.pick_plane()`). Two such places: the void past a room's floor, and a tile room's wall cells, which have no floor quad, so a ray through a wall box that finds no floor behind it lands in the wall's cell. An aim there still points the way the cursor does. (In a layout, the floor under a blocking asset is the layout's choice.)
- **The floor pick** (`WorldView.pick_floor(camera, screen_pos, mask)`, built in P2): a ray from `project_ray_origin/normal()` into the walkable trimesh on 3D layer 1, **two rays a hair apart (0.013, 0.007 screen px), the nearer hit wins**; `Vector3.INF` when neither hits. P0a: one ray through a vertex shared by several triangles went wrong 6–12 times in 20; two, never. P2's `view_test`: a single ray went wrong 3 times in 13 such vertices, and two rays got all 13 exact. On flat floor a plane at y = 0 gives the same point. At 50° pitch and 30° field of view the screen's top edge still looks 35° down, so every pixel's ray meets the floor.
- **Found in P2: a point exactly on an outline is either surface.** Where a raised surface's edge is its outline from the camera (the floor shows right behind it), a screen point exactly on that edge lies between two surfaces. The pick may return the top or the floor just behind, and both are right to the pixel. The second ray only helps where its offset happens to point onto the surface. Nothing in play depends on which: a cursor lands exactly on an outline only by chance, and the next pixel decides.
- **The enemy under the cursor** is picked in screen space (its model's projected height and width), and the aim is then its feet (`global_position`).
  - Built in P5 (`WorldView.unit_at_screen_point()`). A view's box on screen is the bounds of every mesh under its root, at its interpolated (drawn) transform, projected through the camera (`screen_rect_of()`).
  - Where boxes overlap, the box whose center is nearest the cursor wins. Player passes its own filter (targetable enemies).
  - The cursor on the player's own model aims at the floor behind it. Only enemies are picked, with one planned exception (COMPANIONS.md, CO4): while the pressed ability can consume the companion, the companion's view is picked too, by its box the same way. An enemy under the cursor wins over it.

### Engine facts the view relies on (Godot 4.7.2, measured in the spikes)
- `CanvasItem.visibility_layer` + `Viewport.canvas_cull_mask` hide the 2D world without touching its code; visibility layers aren't inherited, and every ancestor must share a layer with the mask.
- `physics/common/physics_interpolation` covers 3D; `Node3D.get_global_transform_interpolated()` exists; Node2D has no interpolated getter.
- In a hand-written `.tres`, a scripted resource's properties must come after its `script = ...` line: a property set before it lands on a plain Resource and is dropped without a warning (P6: five `cast_anim` lines read empty).
- A physics-interpolated Camera3D projects (`unproject_position()`, `project_ray_origin/normal()`) from its interpolated transform outside the physics tick, so a transform set by code shows up late (P3: up to 3,300 px off). A camera moved by code runs with physics interpolation off.
- `Node.process_physics_priority`: lower runs first, whatever the tree order.
- Stretch `canvas_items`: 3D renders at the window's resolution.
- Rays for the floor pick and `unproject_position()` use the 640×360 canvas space.
- `Input.action_press()` sends no input event; scripted ability presses use `Input.parse_input_event()`.
- A SubViewport given the root's `world_2d` draws the same canvas items with its own `canvas_cull_mask` and `canvas_transform` (P7).
- Godot turns processing on at `_ready()` for any script that has `_process`, so a `@tool` script that should process only in the editor turns it off inside `_process()`, not in `_enter_tree()` (P8: marker previews showed in play).
- A tool's look added as an internal child (`add_child(node, false, Node.INTERNAL_MODE_BACK)`) with no owner is never saved into the scene, and `get_children()` / `find_children()` leave internal children out (P8).
- `Geometry2D.convex_hull()` returns its first point again at the end (P8). `RenderingServer.viewport_set_measure_render_time()` with `viewport_get_measured_render_time_gpu()` / `_cpu()` times one viewport on its own.
